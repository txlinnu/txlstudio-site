How much does a website cost in India? What drives the price

By Inayath · 20 Oct 2026 · 5 min read

“How much does a website cost in India?” has no single answer, because the price depends almost entirely on what the site has to do. This guide explains what drives the cost and what to check before you accept a quote. It also uses my own current prices as one real reference point. They are my prices, not an industry average.

The short version
  • Scope sets the price: how many pages, which features, and whether the site sells things online.
  • My current starting prices are ₹5,000 for a Starter website, ₹12,000 for a Standard business site and ₹25,000 for an online store.
  • Domain, hosting and third-party services are usually extra, whoever builds the site.
  • Compare quotes on what is included, not on the headline number.

What actually drives the price

  • Pages and content. A five-page site and a thirty-page site are different projects. So is a site where the text and photos are ready and one where they are not.
  • Custom design or a template. A design made for your brand takes longer than a template with your logo on it, and it looks like it.
  • Features. A contact form is simple. Lead capture, booking, user accounts, a cart and checkout, or a reporting dashboard each add real work.
  • Integrations. Payments, WhatsApp, email tools and anything that connects to your data.
  • Security and speed. Hardening a site and making it load quickly takes effort that cheap quotes often skip.
  • Revisions and timeline. More rounds of changes and a rush deadline both raise the cost.

A real reference: my current prices

These are founding-client rates for my first few clients. The regular price is in brackets. The final quote is always confirmed on a scoping call.

  • Starter website: from ₹5,000 (regular ₹8,000). Up to 5 pages, mobile-responsive, a contact form and basic SEO.
  • Standard website: from ₹12,000 (regular ₹18,000). A full multi-page business site with lead-capture forms, trust sections and a custom design.
  • Online store: from ₹25,000 (regular ₹40,000). Product catalog, cart, checkout and custom interactions, built from scratch.
  • Custom dashboard: from ₹15,000 (regular ₹20,000).
  • AI chatbot: from ₹8,000 (regular ₹12,000) for a basic FAQ bot, and from ₹20,000 (regular ₹30,000) for an advanced one.

Add-ons are ₹1,000 per extra page, ₹2,000 a month for maintenance and support, and +20% for priority delivery. Every build includes a free TXL Aegis security check and 6 months of free bug-fix support. The current prices are always on the pricing page.

What is usually not in the price

  • Domain and hosting. You normally buy and own these yourself, and they are billed separately. That is how I work too.
  • Third-party services. Payment gateway fees, paid plugins, email tools and licenses.
  • Your content. The text, photos and product details usually come from you. Late or missing content is the most common reason a project runs late.
  • Changes after launch. My free 6 months cover bug fixes only. New features, design changes and content updates are quoted separately.

Six questions to ask before you accept any quote

  1. Exactly what is included: how many pages, and which features?
  2. How many rounds of revisions are included? (Mine: two.)
  3. Who owns the code and design afterwards? (Mine: you, once you have paid in full.)
  4. What support do you get after launch, and for how long?
  5. Are the domain, hosting and third-party costs included?
  6. How is payment split? (Mine: a 50% deposit, and the rest before delivery.)

Get the answers in writing. A clear written scope protects both sides.

Warning signs

  • A price with no written scope behind it.
  • No clear answer on who owns the code.
  • No mention of mobile, speed or security.
  • Pressure to pay the full amount up front.

When a page builder is the better choice

For a very simple site, or a quick test of an idea, a builder like Wix or Squarespace can cost less and be perfectly fine. I wrote a separate guide on when custom code is worth it and when it is not.
